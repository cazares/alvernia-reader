## Per-song detail

### #33 "Gloria MSP" — "A ti que estas a la derecha del Padre" → COPYRIGHTED, exclude
- **"MSP" = Misioneros Servidores de la Palabra** (Missionary Servants of the
  Word), NOT "Misa Pascual" as first guessed. The Mass setting is *titled* "Misa
  Pascual"; MSP is the composing/publishing ministry ("Evangelizar Cantando MSP").
- Their channel posts it verbatim as "GLORIA | MISA PASCUAL | MSP" with matching
  incipit + notation. Order erected as Pontifical Institute 2008; monetizes the
  catalog (Spotify ~23K monthly listeners); lay arm asserts "© 2026 … All rights
  reserved."
- Contemporary composed Mass setting, identifiable active rights-holder. The
  Gloria *text* is liturgical/PD, but the *musical setting* is protected.
- Licensing (if ever pursued) runs through the order directly (laec.msp@gmail.com),
  not CCLI/OneLicense.
- Refs: facebook.com/ecantandomsp • misionerosmsp.com • lsp.org.mx • Spotify artist "Misioneros Servidores de la Palabra"

### #41 "Con estas ofrendas (RE)" → ORPHAN, exclude
- Full incipit recovered: **"Con estas ofrendas yo te pido, Señor, que en este
  mundo haya amor…"** — widely-circulated canto de ofertorio.
- Every source shows **no author** and prompts users "¿Sabes quién escribió esta
  canción?" — textbook orphan fingerprint. A stray "Carlos Andalón" attribution
  appeared in ONE search summary but could NOT be corroborated on any actual page
  → treated as unverified.
- **"(RE)" confirmed = key of D major** (LaCuerda chart is in Re), not part of the
  title. Other sources publish it in Do/C.
- Modern (post-1960) piece, untraceable author → orphan → unclearable (not PD).
- Refs: letras.com/canciones-religiosas/con-estas-ofrendas • musica.com • chords.lacuerda.net/mus_catolica/con_estas_ofrendas

### #73 "Cada día que nace" — "Pon tu luz en nuestros ojos oh Señor" → ORPHAN, exclude
- Lyrics confirmed exactly (chorus "Pon tu luz en nuestros ojos oh Señor, para
  saber mirarnos con bondad"; verse "Cada día que nace, nace una flor…").
- No composer/publisher on any source; NOT in OCP, Flor y Canto (3rd/4th), or
  Hymnary.org. Contemporary devotional style (post-1960).
- A lacuerda.net "Signum" performer tag is NOT an author credit (crowd-sourced
  sites mislabel performers as composers).
- Unknown rights-holder + no PD evidence → orphan → exclude.
- Ref: chords.lacuerda.net/signum/cada_dia_que_nace

### #224 "Había olvidado como era mi alma" → COPYRIGHTED, exclude
- Author credited **Osiris Preciado**, recorded by *Cantantes Católicos Gdl.*
  (Guadalajara). Exact-phrase match on the incipit across two independent uploads.
- Earlier "no match" was an accent quirk (*como* vs *cómo*) + it lives on Catholic
  YouTube channels, not mainstream lyric DBs.
- Modern work, living author, no PD/CC dedication → copyrighted → exclude.
- **Caveat:** author attribution rests on the video *titles* (page descriptions
  didn't load), not a registration. Firmer holder-of-record would need the video
  descriptions and/or a SACM (Mexican royalty body) lookup for "Osiris Preciado."
- Ref: youtube.com/watch?v=VEx2oh41Upc (title credits the author)

### #328 "El Señor Resusito" (El Señor Resucitó) — NO incipit → STILL-UNKNOWN, exclude
- **Cannot be disambiguated from the title alone** — at least 4 distinct songs
  share it:
  1. **PD hymn** — "El Señor resucitó, ¡Aleluya! / Muerte y tumba ya venció" —
     trans. Juan Bautista Cabrera (1837–1916), tune EASTER HYMN / Lyra Davidica
     (1708). Hymnary.org labels the text **public domain**.
  2. **Copyrighted** — "Aleluya, el Señor resucitó" — Vicente Morales / Brotes de
     Olivo (group founded 1971).
  3. **Copyrighted** — "Hoy el Señor resucitó… Alegría y paz, hermanos" — modern
     refrain, publisher © (feadulta © 2006).
  4. **Copyrighted arrangement** — OCP bilingual "El Señor Resucitó / Jesus Christ
     Is Risen Today" in Flor y Canto: base Wesley/Lyra Davidica is PD, but OCP's
     Spanish translation + arrangement carry "Derechos reservados."
- For a Catholic parish, a contemporary setting (2/3/4) is arguably MORE likely
  than the Protestant Cabrera hymn → PD cannot be assumed.
- **Subtlety even if it IS the old hymn:** a PD text/tune reproduced via a
  copyrighted MODERN translation or a publisher's ARRANGEMENT (the OCP case) is
  still protected at that layer.
